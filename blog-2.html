Brand Trust & B2B Marketing

Marketing loves the loud and the viral stunts built to break the internet in three seconds. But what happens when the thing you're marketing isn't a snack or a sneaker, and failure isn't a missed meme, but a mismanaged retirement?

On September 21, 2026, Ameriprise Financial launched a new national advertising campaign built on its existing "Advice Worth Talking About" brand platform. Rather than introducing a new idea from scratch, it's a deliberate extension of an established one, using relatable, real-life scenarios to reinforce the value of working with a trusted advisor across digital, social, and podcast sponsorships.

As someone who developed B2B demand-generation and go-to-market strategy at A1Dynamics—working directly with corporate messaging that had to build trust over time rather than chase a single conversion—I look at a campaign like Ameriprise's with real respect. It isn't chasing attention; it's engineering credibility:

- Channel choice signals intent: Podcast sponsorships work differently than a 15-second pre-roll. Hosts build intimate, conversational trust with listeners over hours, not seconds. That's a deliberate fit for a trust-sensitive category, not a default media buy—the exact same reasoning I used when prioritizing channel mix over channel count in my own campaigns.

- Consistency over novelty: Building on an existing platform rather than launching something new is its own strategic statement in high-consideration categories. The brand equity is in repetition, not reinvention.

In high-stakes industries, your creative doesn't need to shout the loudest. It needs to be the clearest, most consistent voice in the room.

How do you balance creative consistency with the pressure to chase new trends? Let's discuss. 👇
